What do spanning trees, graph flows, graph coloring, and network reliability have in common? They can all be studied by a single polynomial! The Tutte polynomial is a remarkably powerful graph invariant that encodes a wide variety of combinatorial information. This talk will explore its construction and some of its evaluations through examples to examine seemingly unrelated information about a graph.

Josephine Reynes
Norwich University

The Game of Cycles is a two-player combinatorial game in which players take turns assigning directions to the edges of a graph, subject to restrictions that prevent the creation of sources and sinks. We introduce the mixed Game of Cycles, which allows some edges to be directed before play begins and permits disconnected graphs. This generalization provides a natural framework for studying game positions using Grundy values and decompositions into simpler games.

We develop structural tools that allow us to determine the Grundy value of any mixed path. In particular, we establish a sharp upper bound and characterize the unique family of mixed paths attaining it. We also introduce a pendant decomposition that yields a simple formula for the Grundy value of any caterpillar graph, depending only on the parity of the number of edges in its spine.

These results illustrate how graph structure can reveal surprisingly simple winning strategies in combinatorial games.

In this talk, we will give a brief history of Ramsey numbers, including recent breakthroughs. Then we will introduce the r-Kneser Ramsey number, which is the minimum n such that every red/blue edge-coloring of the Kneser graph KG(n,r) contains a red clique on s vertices or a blue clique on t vertices. We will give bounds on the r-Kneser Ramsey number as well as some small exact values. This is joint work with Emily Heath, Alex Parker, Coy Schwieder, and Shira Zerbib.
